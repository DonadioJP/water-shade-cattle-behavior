Field Experiment · Florianópolis, Brazil · 2021–2022

Does shade and water access change how cattle behave in a challenging thermal environment?

In pasture-based systems, shade and water are more than comfort resources, they shape when, where, and how long cattle graze. This study measured the behavioral and thermal consequences of restricting both, across two breeds, under real field conditions.

The problem

Cattle in tropical and subtropical pasture systems routinely face periods of intense thermal stress. During the hottest hours of the day, animals face a behavioral trade-off: continue grazing and risk overheating, or seek shade and reduce dry matter intake.

When shade is not available — or when water access is restricted — this trade-off becomes a welfare problem. Animals may reduce voluntary grazing, increase time standing idle, or alter drinking patterns in ways that affect both productivity and wellbeing.

What is less understood is how the combination of shade and water availability shapes cattle behavior and thermal comfort across a full 24-hour cycle. Nocturnal compensatory grazing, drinking dynamics, and body surface temperature responses to restricted resources are rarely studied together.

This experiment set out to answer that directly.

The experiment

Thirty-two cows — 16 Braford and 16 Jersey — were assigned to four treatment combinations across a 4 × 4 Latin square design, managed under Voisin Rational Grazing in silvopastoral paddocks at the Ressacada Experimental Farm, UFSC.

S+W+
Shade available
Water ad libitum 24h
S+W−
Shade available
Water restricted to 1h/day
S−W+
No shade
Water ad libitum 24h
S−W−
No shade
Water restricted to 1h/day

The experiment was conducted at the Ressacada Experimental Farm (FER–UFSC), Florianópolis, Brazil (27°40’25” S; 48°32’30” W), from December 2021 to April 2022.

This research was conducted under the Voisin Rational Grazing system, which structures pasture use around animal welfare and soil recovery.

Shade trees (Inga edulis) were established in rows across experimental paddocks, creating a silvopastoral layout that mirrors real-world agroecological farm conditions in southern Brazil.

Animal procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee on Animal Use (CEUA) of UFSC under Protocol 857122042.

Data collection

  • Black globe thermometers, soil surface measurements, and meteorological station data characterized the thermal environment continuously.
  • Thermographic images captured body surface temperatures at three time points daily.
  • Behavioral scans were recorded every 10 minutes across full 24-hour cycles.
  • Drinking events were recorded continuously.
