Methods

Study design, data collection, and statistical analysis

1 · Study Site and Animals

The study was conducted at the Bovine Vivarium of the Ressacada Experimental Farm (FER), Federal University of Santa Catarina (UFSC), Florianópolis, Brazil (27°40′25″ S; 48°32′30″ W), from December 2021 to April 2022. Animal procedures were approved by the UFSC Ethics Committee on Animal Use (CEUA Protocol 857122042).

Thirty-two females of Braford (478 ± 44 kg) and Jersey (385 ± 33 kg) breeds were used. Animals were individually identified with ear tags and numbered on both flanks with non-toxic paint. The experimental area covered 3.2 ha divided into twelve paddocks of 2,600 m² each.

2 · Experimental Design

The experiment followed a 4 × 4 Latin square design over four 5-day periods (two adaptation days, one rest day, two data collection days), repeated at 30-day intervals. Eight animals (four Braford, four Jersey) were assigned to each of four groups, rotating across treatments each period.

Treatment Code Description
Shade + ad libitum water S+W+ Paddock with shade, pasture, mineral supplement, and water available 24h
Shade + restricted water S+W− Shade and pasture ad libitum; water available 1h/day (12:00–12:30 and 17:30–18:00)
No shade + ad libitum water S−W+ Open paddock with pasture, mineral supplement, and water available 24h
No shade + restricted water S−W− Open paddock; water available 1h/day (12:00–12:30 and 17:30–18:00)

Paddocks were managed under the Voisin Rational Grazing system and subdivided with temporary fencing (1,300 m² per subdivision; 162 m²/animal). Animals were moved daily at 06:50h. Shaded subdivisions provided 390 m² of tree canopy per subdivision (48.7 m²/animal). Six paddocks were shaded by Inga edulis trees planted in rows (3 m between plants; 20 m between rows).

For restricted water treatments, animals were led through a 5 m-wide corridor to troughs located 100 m from the paddocks at the designated times, reflecting practices common on farms without in-paddock water.

3 · Behavioral Observations

Behavioral assessments ran continuously from 07:00 on one day to 07:00 the following day (48h per period). Scan sampling was performed instantaneously every 10 minutes across all four groups simultaneously, yielding 24 scans per hour. Scan 1 = 07:00h; Scan 12 = 18:00h (daytime); Scan 13 = 19:00h; Scan 24 = 06:00h (nighttime). Observers were trained and passed two reliability tests reaching ≥95% agreement before data collection.

Behaviors recorded followed the ethogram of the Laboratory of Applied Ethology (LETA/UFSC):

Behavior Definition
Grazing Head lowered to pasture height, apprehending forage, stationary or moving
Ruminating Lateral mandibular chewing, lying or standing, head at or above body level
Idle standing Standing or lying without body movement
Lying idle Lying without body movement
Lying ruminating Ruminating while lying
Drinking Lips submerged in water with swallowing movements
Other Any behavior not described above

For analysis, behaviors were grouped as:

  • Grazing = grazing only
  • Other = other behaviors (analysed separately; no longer added to grazing)
  • Lying = lying idle + lying ruminating
  • Standing = idle standing + standing ruminating
  • Ruminating = ruminating in any posture (standing + lying)

4 · Drinking Behavior and Water Intake

Drinking events were recorded at each trough visit: animal identity, start and end time, number of sips, and drinking duration (s). Sip rate (sips/s) was derived as sips ÷ duration. Surveillance cameras (Intelbras®) were installed at each trough and recorded continuously during observation days to capture nighttime visits without observer presence. Time spent with head raised mid-bout was discounted from drinking duration.

Daily water intake was measured using water meters (Unimag®, precision 0.01 L) installed at the inlet of each trough, recorded at 07:00 and 19:00h. Consumption volumes were corrected for daily precipitation and evaporation relative to trough surface area. All troughs were supplied from the same source, controlled by a float valve. Daily group intake was divided by 8 to estimate per-animal consumption.

5 · Thermal Comfort

Climatic variables (air temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, precipitation) were collected at the FER–UFSC Meteorological Station (ID 1036), 300 m from the experimental area.

Black globe temperature was recorded every 5 minutes from 08:00 to 18:00h using Data Loggers (Elitech® RC-4HC) installed at 1 m height in shaded and unshaded paddocks.

Wet-Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT) was calculated hourly as a composite thermal stress index for cattle. Thresholds follow Baeta & Souza (2010): ≤ 74 comfort; > 74–78 alert; > 78–84 danger; > 84 critical.

Thermal Radiation Load (TLR) was derived from the Stefan-Boltzmann equation applied to mean radiant temperature (W/m²).

Soil surface temperature was measured hourly from 09:00 to 18:00h using an infrared thermometer (Simpla® TI-55; range −55 to 550°C; precision ±1°C) at three location types: full sun, sun between trees, and under tree canopy (shade). Measurements in shaded paddocks were taken at four shaded and four unshaded points; in unshaded paddocks at four random points. All readings were taken by the same person following a standardized protocol.

6 · Thermography

Non-invasive body surface temperature was assessed for 16 cows (four per group: two Braford, two Jersey). Thermographic images were captured at 3 m distance at 10:00, 13:00, and 16:00h on collection days using an infrared camera (Flir® T640; emissivity = 0.98). Four anatomical regions were assessed: right flank, left flank, eye, and udder.

Images were analyzed in Flir Thermal Studio® to extract minimum, mean, and maximum temperatures per region. Body surface temperature was calculated as (mean + maximum) / 2 following Montanholi et al. (2008); minimum values were excluded due to greater environmental influence. For flank analyses, right and left flank values were averaged per animal, day and capture time.

7 · Statistical Analysis

All analyses were performed in R (R Core Team, 2023) using lme4, lmerTest, and emmeans. Code is available via the Code buttons on each results page.

Experimental unit and random structure

The design is a 4 × 4 Latin square (4 groups of 8 cows × 4 periods, 2 collection days per period). The experimental unit is group × period (16 units), not the animal-hour, so every animal-level model includes a group × period random effect in addition to group, animal and day/hour. Period is a fixed effect and group is random:

\[ Y = \text{treatment} + \text{period} + (1 \mid \text{group}) + (1 \mid \text{group:period}) + (1 \mid \text{animal\_id}) + (1 \mid \text{day} / \text{hour}) + \varepsilon \]

Models that did not converge or were singular were refitted with the bobyqa optimizer; random terms whose variance was estimated as zero were then dropped (never group × period). Each results page lists the models affected.

Linear mixed models (LMM)

Continuous outcomes were analyzed with lmer() (lmerTest), with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom for all tests and EMMs. Behavioral outcomes (scans/h of grazing, other, lying, standing and ruminating) were modelled separately for daytime and nighttime. Drinking duration was modelled on the log scale and is reported as back-transformed geometric means. Water intake is a group-day measure (group intake ÷ 8) and was modelled with group and group × period as random effects (day within group × period is the residual). Thermography models used flank temperature averaged over the right and left flank, with treatment and capture time in the same model, and (1 | group) + (1 | group:period) + (1 | animal_id) + (1 | day).

Generalized linear mixed models (GLMM)

Daily drinking events per animal were counted on the complete animal × day grid (animal-days without drinking entered as 0) and modelled with a Poisson GLMM (glmer()). The Pearson dispersion ratio was checked; when it was clearly below 1, a generalized Poisson GLMM (glmmTMB, genpois) was fitted as a sensitivity check. Sips per drinking event were modelled with a Poisson GLMM, switching to a negative binomial GLMM (glmmTMB, nbinom2) when overdispersed. Back-transformed rates and rate ratios are reported.

Factorial models

The four treatments form a 2 × 2 factorial. Each outcome (except shade use) was also modelled as shade * water + period with the same random structure, and shade, water and shade × water were tested with emmeans::joint_tests(). Where the interaction had p < 0.10, simple effects (shade within water, water within shade) are reported.

Experimental-unit sensitivity analysis

As a check on the mixed models, every behavior, drinking and intake outcome was averaged to one value per group × period (16 values) and analysed as a classic Latin square with lm(): y ~ treatment + period + group and y ~ shade * water + period + group (6 error df). Coat color and breed, which vary only between animals, were tested in mixed models that keep the animal random effect and also on one mean per animal (n = 16).

Estimated marginal means and comparisons

Estimated marginal means (EMMs) and pairwise contrasts were obtained using the emmeans package with Tukey adjustment for multiple comparisons. Model-based results are reported as EMM ± SE; descriptive tables report raw mean ± SD. Significance is indicated as: *** p < 0.001; ** p < 0.01; * p < 0.05; . p < 0.1; ns p ≥ 0.1. Residual diagnostics (residuals vs fitted and QQ plots for LMMs; DHARMa simulated residuals for GLMMs) are shown in collapsible sections on each results page.

Shade use

Proportion of daytime scans recorded in shade was modeled as a continuous outcome using LMM, restricted to S+ treatments (S+W+ and S+W−). The 12:00h and 17:00h scan blocks, when W− animals were taken to the trough corridor (12:00h and 17:30–18:00h), were excluded for both S+ treatments.
